Many people drawn to New Zealand's pristine landscapes often overlook Auckland on the North Island, rushing straight to the South Island, the outdoor adventurer's paradise.

In Auckland, what people take the most pride in is not working themselves to the bone, turning themselves into economic animals — but the pastoral life they have defended and created. Wooden houses, the sea, gardens — these are the essence of living in New Zealand. Sipping coffee at a streetside cafe, jogging along a coastal path, driving out to find a quiet fishing dock...

The Aucklander's life: **their healthy, sun-kissed skin, strolling casually along the beach, a lazy afternoon nap on the lawn.**

The passion for life that radiates from the bones of Aucklanders is deeply infectious. They channel the bulk of their intelligence and wealth into enjoying life. If you are visiting Auckland for the first time, this city may very well recalibrate the direction of your own life. That is its magic.

New Zealanders call themselves Kiwis, a nickname derived from the country's national bird, a flightless, peculiar creature.

Auckland — half city, half sea. Countless islands and volcanoes. Sunshine, beaches, the scent of the ocean in the air. Docks crammed with luxury yachts. This is New Zealand's largest **port city.**

Auckland has a tantalizing Maori name: Tāmaki Makau Rau. In the Maori language, it means "the pure maiden with a hundred lovers," also translated as "the isthmus of a thousand lovers." This speaks to Auckland's eminently livable environment.

Centuries ago, Auckland's beauty already drew many tribes to fight over it. Its beauty might be described as "unadorned radiance, needing no powder or paint." **So why not come and be captivated by its charm?**

**◇ 9:00 AM Gazing at a Thousand Sails from the Wharf**

![Auckland marina](images/016.jpg)

Jinyi, Qyer

Aucklanders have three great passions: "the beach, boating, and barbecues."

Walking along the shore, countless sails line up in dense formation — a magnificent sight.

It is said that, on average, one in every three Auckland residents owns a yacht. On sunny weekends, locals take to the sea, sailing to any nearby island to while away a day of pure contentment.

![Divider](images/017.png)

Ferry Building

99 Quay Street, Auckland Central

![Ferry Building](images/018.jpg)

Fengqingyang, Mafengwo

Not far from the wharf, you will spot a striking bright yellow Baroque-style building — the Ferry Building, one of Auckland's iconic port landmarks. From a distance, the building's plinth is granite, while the upper portion is constructed of red brick and sandstone, with red and yellow alternating. The iron railings outside and the kerosene lamps inside are deeply vintage. The entire structure is like a unique work of art.

The building was completed in 1912 and originally served as the British Customs House, quietly accompanying this city through many years.

In 1982, the New Zealand Historic Places Trust listed the Ferry Building as a heritage site and carried out a major restoration between 1986 and 1988. Today, its ground floor is mostly shops and cafes, while most ferry services have moved to the new terminal.

![Divider](images/017.png)

Queen Street

Queen Street, Auckland Central

![Queen Street](images/019.jpg)

Fengqingyang, Mafengwo

Leaving the Ferry Building and heading south, you enter Auckland's downtown, the bustling commercial district of Queen Street. Here, an array of shops, department stores, restaurants, and hotels converge — the liveliest part of the city.

Queen Street was laid out in 1840 and named after Queen Victoria. The architecture along the street is a highlight: besides the Auckland Ferry Building, there is the 19th-century Greek Revival-style Bank of New Zealand Building and the Auckland Town Hall.

Vintage heritage buildings stand shoulder to shoulder with modern skyscrapers — a bustling district that carries an air of solemnity.

![Divider](images/017.png)

Sky Tower

Corner Victoria St W and Federal St, Auckland Central

![Sky Tower](images/020.jpg)

Carl Madsen on Twitter

Walking along Queen Street, you will spot Auckland's landmark Sky Tower from afar. In truth, you can see it from virtually anywhere in the city center — it feels like you will never get lost as long as you can see it.

![Sky Tower from street](images/021.jpg)

saviourwin, Qyer

At the intersection of Queen Street and Victoria Street West, turn onto Victoria Street West and walk uphill for about 300 meters, and you will arrive at its base.

The Sky Tower stands 328 meters tall, the tallest structure in the Southern Hemisphere to date. From the observation deck at the top, a panoramic view of Auckland unfolds — whether by day, at dusk, or at night, the scenery is breathtaking. Thrill-seekers can also try the "SkyWalk" and "SkyJump," sending their adrenaline soaring among the clouds.

**◇ 11:00 AM Lunch High Above the City**

The Sky Tower, the tallest building in the Southern Hemisphere, took two years and nine months to construct.

Ascending the tower feels like floating among the clouds. Add a delicious meal, and you can savor Auckland's cityscape one bite, one view at a time — nothing could be more wonderful.

![Orbit revolving restaurant](images/022.jpg)

Web image

The Orbit Revolving Restaurant sits atop the Sky Tower, 190 meters above ground, completing a full 360-degree rotation every hour.

Sitting by the window, enjoying New Zealand cuisine while overlooking Auckland's beauty — a feast for both the eyes and the stomach. The prices are moderate, the dishes are delicious, and dining here grants you free access to the Sky Tower's main observation deck. Tables are in high demand, so book at least three days in advance.

![Orbit restaurant food](images/023.jpg)

Web image

When in Auckland, you absolutely must not miss the local seafood: crayfish, oysters, and green-lipped mussels — tender, succulent flesh in rich, flavorful broth.

**◇ 13:00 PM A Dose of Culture After Lunch**

![Divider](images/017.png)

University of Auckland

22 Princes St, Auckland

![University of Auckland clock tower](images/024.jpg)

introlaughing, Qyer

Walking from Queen Street through Albert Park, a dreamy white clock tower appears before you.

This is the University of Auckland.

The University of Auckland is a world-renowned institution and the most influential seat of learning in New Zealand. No gates, no walls — the entire campus seems to be nestled within a garden, tranquil and beautiful. Strolling through the grounds, watching students come and go across the lawns and tree-lined avenues, you are met with a wave of youthful vitality. This must be the campus of everyone's dreams.

![Divider](images/017.png)

Auckland Domain

20 Park Road, Auckland

![Auckland Domain](images/025.jpg)

Zhufeng Xiaoxiao, Sina Blog

After visiting the university, head southeast and you will see vast expanses of green — that is Auckland Domain. The Domain covers an enormous area, with sprawling lawns and wooded groves.

This is Aucklanders' favorite weekend retreat. In the mornings, many come here to exercise. On sunny weekends, families bring their children to play football, walk their dogs — relaxed and carefree. If you drive up through the park, you may even spot herds of cattle and sheep grazing contentedly, along with other small wildlife.

Wandering through this urban garden, your entire body and mind unwind. One cannot help but envy the slow pace of Aucklanders' lives.

![Divider](images/017.png)

Auckland War Memorial Museum

The Auckland Domain, Parnell, Auckland

![Auckland War Memorial Museum](images/026.jpg)

TINTINANDSNOWY, Qyer

On a small hill within the Domain, you will see a white building, with a football field and running track below. Follow the path uphill and you will arrive at the Auckland Museum, also known as the Auckland War Memorial Museum.

The museum is a neoclassical Gothic-style building, richly furnished across three floors. It is a comprehensive museum housing New Zealand's historical and ethnographic artifacts. The highlight is the indigenous Maori culture section, with three Maori cultural performances each day — well worth watching.

After the museum, stroll back down the hill and leave the ancient Auckland Domain behind. A lazy afternoon pairs perfectly with dessert — a warm cup of tea and a delicious pastry, savoring the slow rhythm of leisure time.

**◇ 16:00 PM The Pavlova You Must Try**

![Pavlova dessert](images/027.jpg)

New Zealand's dairy products are world-renowned, and the Pavlova — a cream-and-fruit meringue — is the country's most iconic dessert. It is named after the famous Russian ballerina Anna Pavlova, who toured the Southern Hemisphere in the 1920s. It is said that New Zealanders created this dessert to honor the great dancer.

Pavlova is a meringue-based cake: a crisp outer shell of baked meringue, a soft, pillowy filling, topped with a thick layer of fresh cream and generously adorned with seasonal fruits. It looks utterly delightful just to behold.

Many restaurants across the city serve this dessert. You can try it at the aforementioned Orbit restaurant, or head to Euro Restaurant & Bar near Viaduct Harbour (@ Shed 22 Princes Wharf, Auckland) — one of Auckland's finest — to taste a Pavlova paired with kiwifruit and Lemon & Paeroa (New Zealand's wildly popular soft drink), an unexpectedly delightful flavor experience.

**◇ 17:00 PM Feel the Sea Breeze**

![Divider](images/017.png)

Mission Bay

Mission Bay, Auckland

![Mission Bay](images/028.jpg)

Jinyi, Qyer

As a coastal city, the deep blue sea is visible wherever you look. Following the scent of the ocean, you find yourself at the beach without even trying. Azure waters, a gentle sea breeze — all your worries drift away.

![Mission Bay beach](images/029.jpg)

Jinyi, Qyer

Mission Bay is the closest beach to downtown Auckland — just a ten-minute bus ride away. On weekends, bringing the whole family here to barbecue and soak up the sun is a favorite Auckland pastime.

To the west, you can see the Harbour Bridge. Directly north lies Rangitoto Island. To the northeast, Waiheke Island can be glimpsed in the distance. And of course, the Sky Tower is visible everywhere.

On sunny days, the beach is filled with sunbathers — men and women, young and old, from every corner of the world. If you meet friendly locals, they might even invite you to join their picnic, letting you experience the warmth and inclusiveness of this land.

**◇ 19:00 PM Lingering in the Sunset Glow**

![Divider](images/017.png)

Mount Eden

260 Mount Eden Road, Auckland Central

![Mount Eden crater](images/030.jpg)

saviourwin, Qyer

In the evening, seek out a perfect spot to watch the sunset. As the sun slowly sinks, in the lingering glow of dusk, the heart softens along with it.

![Mount Eden sunset](images/031.jpg)

Kenny Muir Photography

Auckland is dotted with volcanoes, and Mount Eden is the tallest of its many volcanic cones — the highest natural point in the city. Today, the bottom of the volcanic crater is a lush expanse of green, a truly magical sight. At 196 meters above sea level, the summit offers an unobstructed view of the city and the surrounding sea — including the Sky Tower, One Tree Hill Domain, and Rangitoto Island.

It is the best free spot to take in Auckland's panorama, and at sunset, it is especially intoxicating.

![Mount Eden panoramic view](images/032.jpg)

saviourwin, Qyer

One day — how fleeting it is. If you are willing to give Auckland more time, you can venture out to the many nearby islands. Among them, Waiheke Island is where the poet Gu Cheng spent his final years in seclusion (@124 Fairview Cres, Rocky Bay).

"The dark night gave me dark eyes, yet I use them to seek the light." Sadly, in the end, he chose to leave this world with his wife, casting a faint veil of sorrow over this island.

Fans of Gu Cheng can take the ferry to the island and see the utopia through the poet's eyes.

**Auckland's beauty is not about how stunning it is. It is a natural, relaxed attitude toward life.** Simply feeling the sea breeze, watching locals play on the sand — that alone is deeply moving.
