**1 Tong Ah Eating House / 9:00**

![Tong Ah Eating House storefront](images/003.jpg)

To start the day, I fell in love with a classic Nanyang breakfast: kaya toast, coffee, and soft-boiled eggs.

This tiny, unassuming spot has been serving authentic, no-frills kaya toast since 1939 — a rare feat. The white bread, soaked in egg and pan-fried to a light crisp, is paired with the shop's homemade kaya jam, a labor of love that the owner spends eight hours preparing by hand. The chef spreads the jam in under ten seconds, and the toast arrives at your table piping hot. Paired with a robust cup of traditional Nanyang kopi, this buttery treat is a match made in heaven. I'm starting to envy Singaporeans a little.

Address: 35 Keong Saik Rd, Singapore 089142

Phone: +65 6223 5083

Hours: Daily 7 AM – 10 PM

**2 Tiong Bahru Market / 10:00**

![Tiong Bahru Market food](images/004.jpg)

*ins@bibikgourmand*

I love Tiong Bahru. It is not a grand spectacle, but it is one of the city's most charming hidden corners.

![Cycling in Tiong Bahru](images/005.jpg)

Cycling through this artsy neighborhood is the perfect way to explore at a leisurely pace. Winding through its narrow streets, you will discover Singapore's oldest public housing estates alongside a crop of modern cafes, quirky independent bookstores, and trendy new lifestyle shops.

![Tiong Bahru street food](images/006.jpg)

*ins@lefooding*

After the ride, you will likely be hungry. Head to the nearby Tiong Bahru Market. "Mostly locals come here to eat and shop for produce. The national comfort foods are abundant: chwee kueh (steamed rice cakes), rojak, prawn-and-rib noodle soup, and — a must for summer — a glass of freshly pressed sugarcane juice, not too sweet, just the right kind of refreshing. Paired with a good meal, it's just right."

Address: 30 Seng Poh Road, Singapore 168898

**3 Plain Vanilla / 11:00**

![Plain Vanilla cupcake shop](images/007.jpg)

Before leaving the neighborhood, you will pass a bakery that radiates happiness — worth a quick peek inside. It offers a dazzling array of European-style cupcakes, each one so beautiful it almost feels wrong to eat: red velvet, salted caramel, chocolate, Earl Grey, peanut butter cream... every flavor a tiny work of art.

![Plain Vanilla cupcakes](images/008.jpg)

*ins@pvbakery*

The shop is petite and charming, yet it holds the crown as "the city's top cupcake destination." From day one, its mission has been pure and simple: to offer old-fashioned, honest baked goods. A local friend recommends the lavender Earl Grey cupcake.

Address: Tiong Bahru Estate 1D Yong Siak Street, Singapore 168641

Phone: +65 8363 7614

Hours: Mon – Sat 8 AM – 7 PM

**4 Gardens By The Bay / 12:00**

![Gardens by the Bay](images/009.jpg)

*ins@msyan_*

Past noon, Singapore's tropical warmth becomes unmistakable — the sun blazing, the city's heat embracing you. I always look for a cool indoor sanctuary.

The moment you step inside, amid the rainforest, mist, and humidity, Singapore unveils itself as the true "Garden City" — and this is the cool, refreshing interlude I want to share with you.

![Cloud Forest at Gardens by the Bay](images/010.jpg)
*ins@gardensbythebay*

If you love plants as much as I do, you will fall completely in love with this place. Seeing flora from California, South Africa, Australia, and the Mediterranean gardens makes me nostalgic for past journeys.

I was awestruck by the Supertree Grove, the world's tallest indoor waterfall — it felt like stepping onto another planet. I adore the Flower Dome and the Supertree Grove, but especially the Cloud Forest, ethereal as a fairyland. Breathtakingly beautiful.

Address: 18 Marina Gardens Drive

Phone: +65 6420 6848

**5 National Gallery / 15:00**

![National Gallery Singapore](images/011.jpg)

By three in the afternoon, the Lion City's sunlight softens. I would love to take you to the National Gallery. "When you visit a city or a country, you should go to its art museum first. It is there that you can glimpse the soul, the sacredness, and the sense of justice of that place."

![National Gallery interior dome](images/012.jpg)

As a photographer, I also find joy in discovering architectural beauty on my travels. Here, white glass domes and gorgeous corner courtyards bathed in skylight. The intense light and shadow of Southeast Asia fall gently upon the elegant colonial-era buildings — soft and radiant.

![National Gallery courtyard](images/013.jpg)

*ins@dreachong*

Address: 1 St. Andrew's Road, National Gallery Singapore, Singapore 178957

Phone: +65 6271 7000

**6 Cats Socrates / 17:00**

![Cats Socrates bookstore](images/014.jpg)

*ins@cat_socrates*

Those who follow my travel stories know that I love browsing quirky, one-of-a-kind shops. Cats Socrates, this delightful little curiosity shop, is one from my private list that I want to share with you — and I think you will love its vibe.

The shop is filled with cat-themed objects, along with books and magazines on photography, film, art, and lifestyle aesthetics, independent music records, and creative curios. It also regularly hosts fun photo exhibitions, intimate concerts, film screenings, and other indie cultural events.

![Cats Socrates interior](images/015.jpg)

Behind every shop with character is a soulful story. The owners, Zhang Zhen and his wife Jiang Hanglei, are Shanghainese dream-chasers. Originally fashion designers who had always wanted to open a shop, they finally realized their dream after settling in Singapore, creating a space dedicated to Chinese-language literary books — and its popularity has only grown.

The shop's name was indeed inspired by an actual cat, named Socrates. A cat of distinguished cultural taste, one might say.

![Socrates the cat](images/016.jpg)

Address: #03-39B Bras Basah Complex, 231 Bain Street, Singapore 180231

Website: http://www.catsocrates.com.sg/

Contact: +65 6333 0870; contact@catsocrates.com.sg

**7 Timbre / 19:00**

![Timbre live house](images/017.jpg)

In the evening, I love going to live houses to catch local bands. At Timbre, the vocalists are worth hearing, and fans of heavy metal and rock will feel especially at home. You can even request your favorite songs.

It is great whether you go alone or with friends, and the food is reasonably priced. I am particularly fond of one delicious pizza — a local twist of roasted duck topped with crispy popiah skin.

Phone: +65 6336 3386

Every time I do one of these little interviews, my kaleidoscopically rich circle of friends never ceases to amaze me.

This time, I interviewed **several** cool cycling-loving friends and asked them about their unique ways of unlocking a city.

They told me about cycling through Singapore and different corners of the world — **what it really feels like to explore a city on two wheels**. After hearing their stories, I felt genuinely envious.

**@Linda**

![Linda avatar](images/020.png)

Keywords: Seaside castles | Hidden pocket parks | Sunset | Beer | Sea breeze | Clean

It was 2011, on the eve of Christmas. My boyfriend at the time (now my ex) and I cycled along Singapore's East Coast, tracing the shoreline, feeling the sea breeze and the beauty of nature. Along the way we encountered charming little restaurants, bars, and convenience stores. What stood out most were the hidden pocket parks and miniature botanical gardens we stumbled upon.

We stayed until nearly sunset, bought a few cans of beer, and sat on the beach building sandcastles, watching distant cruise ships and boats, hearing the laughter of people and children nearby. Soaking in the clean, orderly impression left by one of the cleanest cities in the world.

**@Dabaibai**

![Dabaibai avatar](images/020.png)

Keywords: Seaside castles | Hidden pocket parks | Sunset | Beer | Sea breeze | Clean

In the Netherlands, my daily commute is by bike. But I have also cycled while traveling in Munich, Copenhagen, and Maastricht.

My must-stop spots are: beautiful bike shops, cafes, yogurt shops, bookstores, furniture stores, and design studios. My girlfriend, meanwhile, will pause at practically every accessories shop.

About 30% of the places I visit are ones I had heard about beforehand, were recommended by friends, or found through research. The other 70% are discovered around the first 30%. I tend to take things slowly — the good places I find on the first day, I love to revisit on the second.

What's unique is that I always seek out bicycle cafes first. Beyond meeting fellow cyclists and finding fun merchandise and accessories, the staff or owner can usually recommend cycling-friendly routes, great neighborhoods, or bars worth riding to. If a bike shop owner is interesting enough, I will ask if there are any group rides I can join.

**@Young**

![Young avatar](images/020.png)

Keywords: Chinatown | Mosque | Eclectic styles | Born and bred

I cycled in Singapore once. The most special discovery was that cycling showed me this place is about so much more than shopping and Universal Studios.

The roads were uncrowded, and the temperature was pleasantly cool. I cycled through Chinatown, the Arab Quarter, past mosques and Buddhist temples, alongside impossibly tall skyscrapers, impeccably manicured gardens, and grand colonial buildings. The styles were incredibly diverse. I stopped at a lovely Hainanese eatery for tea and snacks, and chatted with some born-and-bred Singaporeans who answered all the questions we had along the way.

**@Ghhhh**

![Ghhhh avatar](images/020.png)

Keywords: Wind in the trees | Barking dogs | Fragrant flowers | Off the beaten path | Local life

I once cycled in Giethoorn. It felt like a paradise away from the city's clamor — a village of canals, little bridges, and winding footpaths.

Though every guidebook insists you must explore by boat, my cycling-loving companion and I chose bicycles instead. Because I have always believed: **take a different path, and you will see different scenery.**

The weather was gorgeous that day. I could feel the wind brushing past my ears, hear dogs barking from courtyards, and smell the fragrance of flowers along the way. It reminded me of the classical verse: "A winding path leads to a secluded spot, where a Zen temple hides among flowers and trees." A great journey is not just seen with the eyes — it must be felt with every other sense.

The rental shop owner gave us a map. We followed it loosely, but still occasionally veered off course. The locals were warm and welcoming — even a man repairing his roof paused to wave and greet us.

My reason for loving bicycles: if you want to truly understand a place, cycling is the best choice. It keeps a good pace for exploration while conserving energy; the bike is compact, and not too demanding on the road. When conditions allow, **wandering left and right, soaking in the local rhythm of life.**

**@Vicky**

![Vicky avatar](images/020.png)

Keywords: Picnic | Good food | Overlooked scenery | Relaxation and enjoyment

Whenever I arrive in a major city on my travels, if possible, I always take a bike tour. It has become a habit.

Cycling not only helps you get your bearings faster, but it also lets you see the scenery you would miss from a train or a taxi.

What makes cycling in Singapore fun is that you can see the city from a local's perspective.

That day the cycling path was wonderfully cool and the ride was relaxing. I wandered through all kinds of residential neighborhoods and historic ethnic districts, breezing past tourist spots like Little India, the Singapore Flyer, and Marina Bay Sands. We even ended with a picnic, and the food was delicious. Everything was pure, unadulterated enjoyment. By the time I left, I was seriously thinking about moving to Singapore to work for a few years!

**@Junna**

![Junna avatar](images/020.png)

Keywords: Adventure | River turtle | Map shop | Light rain

I had a cycling experience in Ottawa. It was drizzling that day. With a waterproof map in hand, I paid no attention to any "must-see" lists and just rode wherever felt right. The roads rolled up and down, not too wide — it felt like an adventure.

Along the river, I saw a large turtle stranded onshore. Someone called the authorities, and they helped it back into the water.

Passing the Parliament buildings, they stood like petrified policemen in the rain — less graceful, in that moment, than a small chapel nearby.

I passed a map shop, filled from floor to ceiling with every version and form of world map imaginable — some marked with animals, some with weapons. Whatever you wanted, you could find a world map to suit your taste.

Once a capital sheds its identity as an economic center, it can be truly delightful.

**@Weibuzudao**

![Weibuzudao avatar](images/020.png)

Keywords: Houhai Lake | Kindred spirits | One Night in Beijing | Sunshine and blue skies

On Sundays, I usually hop on a shared bike (the yellow ones, haha) to explore the hutong alleys around Houhai. I had once heard Chen Sheng's song "One Night in Beijing," and the lyrics mentioned an alley called "Baihua Shenchu" — "Deep in the Flowers." So I made a point of searching for it.

When you cycle through the hutongs and meet others who love riding and alley-wandering just as much, it feels like an instant bond. You encounter so many scenes worth preserving in memory: a girl dropping a postcard into a mailbox, a musician playing guitar and singing by the Drum Tower, an old man smoking and chatting in the alleyway, the sun bright, the sky impossibly blue, and Shichahai Lake just as blue.

I admit, after reading all these stories,

I have decided to cycle more often when I travel the world.

After all, the journey ahead, away from the crowds and distractions, is a private experience that belongs only to you.

After all, in the moments when a city breeze brushes past, we temporarily shed our cool, aloof urban faces and become gentle again.
