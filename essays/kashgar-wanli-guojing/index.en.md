# Kashgar, Xinjiang · Notes on *Ten Thousand Miles of Borderland* Season 2

> China's westernmost frontier city and a jewel of the ancient Silk Road. These are my thoughts after watching episode 3 of the documentary *Ten Thousand Miles of Borderland*. For the full story with photos, follow the link to my original Zhihu post.

## About This Story

*Ten Thousand Miles of Borderland* is a documentary series I deeply admire; season 2 is set in Xinjiang. Guest Yang Le explores Kashgar with locals — wandering the old town, creative shops and the great livestock bazaar to experience the charm of a different culture. I watched episode 3 and was moved to write about it.

The story starts with "where is Kashgar": on the northern slopes of the Pamir Plateau, at the western edge of the Taklamakan Desert, bordered by eight countries with 888.5 km of frontier. It moves into the Kashgar old town — the only surviving "labyrinth-style" ancient city in China, over 2,000 years old and a filming location for *The Kite Runner* — and introduces Maylan Turak, a 90s-born young man who opened a creative shop in the old town. Then come the bazaars: the hat bazaar, the herb bazaar, the copper bazaar, the instrument bazaar, and the Kashgar livestock bazaar, the largest live-animal market in Asia.

The story also reaches the westernmost border: Tashkurgan County, China's only border county bordering three countries, the world's highest border post at Khunjerab Pass (4,733 m), and the Khunjerab border police station where season 2 begins. Watching, you see the majestic beauty of the frontier and read of ordinary heroes' dedication and inheritance.
