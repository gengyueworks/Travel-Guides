# Majorelle Garden, Marrakesh

> The garden beloved by Yves Saint Laurent, one of the most mysterious gardens of the 20th century. This story is built around photographs — for the full experience, follow the link to my original Zhihu post.

## About the Garden

The Jardin Majorelle is located on Yves Saint Laurent Avenue in Marrakesh, Morocco. In 1923, painter Jacques Majorelle bought the land and treated the garden as his canvas, building an impressionist garden out of bold shapes and colours. The project drained his fortune, and he was forced to sell parts of it off.

Yves Saint Laurent and his partner Pierre Bergé fell in love with the garden on their first visit to Morocco in 1966, stopped its demolition, bought it, and began a long and costly restoration. Of all its colours, one stands above the rest: "Majorelle Blue, 37.6% red, 31.4% green, 86.3% blue." Saint Laurent used it in his mascara, nail polish and fringed high heels. His ashes rest in the garden's rose garden.

Step through Marrakesh's scorching afternoon air into the garden, and you enter a feast for the senses: cactus gardens, vine corridors, lotus ponds and bamboo paths, fragrant with plants and running water. The museum dome inside is painted like a starry sky — worth lingering beneath.
