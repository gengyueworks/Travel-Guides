05 ◇ City Checklist

Korea is not a country blessed with abundant ingredients. It mostly comes down to just a few categories. Rice dishes: dolsot bibimbap, gimbap. Noodles: ramyeon, naengmyeon, instant noodles. Hot pots: budae jjigae, samgyetang, galbitang, haemultang. Meat: bulgogi, bossam. And of course, every dish is accompanied by an array of **banchan (side dishes) — this, to me, is the essence of Korean cuisine. So what is actually worth eating?** In 72 hours, I sampled around 30 different classic Korean dishes — no repeats — while also covering Seoul's iconic neighborhoods. If I had to recommend some, here is what I would tell you is worth your while.

**●○ Dongdaemun**

The Mega Wholesale Market

Dongdaemun is Korea's wholesale clothing market. It is not just a fabric market — it is the sourcing hub for countless Taobao Korean-style shops. Think of it as Seoul's answer to Beijing's Dongwuyuan wholesale market, and the image will immediately come to life.

But it is not all cheap clothing. If you are up for shopping, Doota here is worth a visit — a five-story mall that gathers many designer brands and familiar labels, with the most impressive feature being that it stays open until 5 AM.

Since I stayed at the JW Marriott in Dongdaemun on this trip, I naturally ate around the neighborhood more often, and gained some insight into Dongdaemun's food scene. So let Seoul's first food zone begin right here.

**Korean BBQ | 하루연가**

![하루연가 location](images/009.png)

하루연가

198-2 Hyehwa-dong, Jongno-gu, Seoul

Korean BBQ is a must, and this restaurant was recommended by the Korean staff at the hotel — a place where young locals often gather. BBQ is a quintessential Korean dish, especially suited for family gatherings. Beef, pork, and pork belly sizzle and crackle over charcoal, lively and festive.

![Korean BBQ grill](images/010.jpg)

Every BBQ restaurant provides garlic cloves, doenjang (soybean paste), pepper, salt, and other condiments. The grilled meat, dipped in seasoning and wrapped in fresh lettuce — a deeply satisfying mouthful.

![Korean BBQ meat](images/011.jpg)

At **하루연가**, the meat selection is abundant: beef, Jeju black pork, pork belly — all of excellent quality. The beef, in particular, does not need to be cooked through; eating it medium brings out a mouthful of rich beefy flavor, utterly gratifying. Paired with authentic Korean makgeolli (rice wine), it becomes a genuine indulgence.

![Korean rice wine with BBQ](images/012.jpg)

**Rib Soup | 유가네닭갈비 동대문점**

![유가네닭갈비 location](images/009.png)

유가네닭갈비 동대문점

동대문 디자인플라자&파크, Seoul

Koreans love their soups: doenjang jjigae, budae jjigae, samgyetang, haemultang, galbitang — the list goes on. This is partly due to Korea's limited ingredients: toss everything into one pot and stew it together, accompanied by various banchan and rice, and it becomes a lively, communal meal.

This restaurant serves not only galbitang (rib soup) but also budae jjigae (army stew). The ribs are large, meaty bones, simmered in spicy kimchi broth until deeply flavorful. Perfect for a winter evening with a few close friends and a bubbling pot.

![Korean rib soup](images/013.jpg)

**Sandwich | Issac Toast & Coffee**

![Issac Toast location](images/009.png)

Issac Toast & Coffee

198-2 Hyehwa-dong, Jongno-gu, Seoul

Seoul, always passionate about culinary innovation, has transformed the traditional toast, egg, and meat combination into a wildly popular breakfast among young people. The chain is everywhere in Seoul — whether in bustling Myeongdong or student-heavy Hongdae, you will spot an Issac Toast & Coffee.

![Issac Toast sandwich](images/014.jpg)

Griddled toast, layered with a square-shaped egg, cheese, and a meat patty, topped with shredded cabbage, a squeeze of ketchup, and mayonnaise — it becomes a sweet, savory, tangy, crunchy toast bursting with layered flavors.

**Night Market | Dongdaemun Night Market**

![Dongdaemun night market location](images/009.png)

Dongdaemun Square, Seoul

Another lively spot in Dongdaemun is the row of street stalls lining the front of the shopping malls, selling authentic Korean street food classics: sundae (blood sausage), tteokbokki (spicy rice cakes), braised snacks, dumplings, and desserts.

![Korean street food](images/016.jpg)

Like in many Chinese cities, these street foods are the living, breathing embodiment of local life. However, with more and more Chinese visitors, many of these stalls now display signs reading "RMB Accepted."

**Dessert | REMICONE Thunder Cloud Ice Cream**

![REMICONE location](images/009.png)

REMICONE

서울특별시 강남구 신사동 547-12 1층, Seoul

The Thunder Cloud ice cream is one of Seoul's most sought-after desserts right now — its unique shape makes it irresistible. The recipe is actually not complicated: a base of milk ice cream, topped with a cloud-shaped puff of cotton candy, sprinkled with glittering gold powder. **●○ Sinsa-dong Garosu-gil**

A Hub of Designer Shops and Fun Desserts

Garosu-gil in Sinsa-dong, Gangnam, **is my favorite place — a gathering of designer boutiques, interesting cafes and dessert shops, and fun vintage stores.** The shopping experience here is far better than Dongdaemun or Myeongdong, and the food scene is younger and fresher — especially the desserts. **Seoul's most fun and playful desserts can all be found here:** Deux Cremes, a favorite for celebrity wrap parties; Sulbing, which perfectly marries ice cream and cake; Amorino, where gelato is sculpted into flower petals. Every dessert is exquisite and delicious — almost too perfect for your social media feed.

**Rice Wine | Chez Maak**

![Chez Maak location](images/009.png)

Chez Maak

강남구 도산대로11길 22 (가로수길), 신사동, Seoul

For the first restaurant in this area, I want to introduce this directly-operated restaurant from an 80-year-old Korean brewery. Here, you can not only drink the brewery's own liquor but also enjoy excellent bibimbap.

![Chez Maak bibimbap](images/019.jpg)

Koreans love bibimbap because **it can quickly replenish energy and vitamins in a short time.** But Chez Maak's version is not the traditional stone pot bibimbap. Like the other establishments in this neighborhood, it has been adapted into something closer to Japanese donburi — beef or pork layered over rice, drizzled with sauce and Korean seasonings. Mixed together, it makes for a uniquely different donburi experience.

![Chez Maak interior](images/020.jpg)

Sulbing

강남구 강남대로152길 45 (가로수길점), 신사동, Seoul

![Amorino location](images/009.png)

Amorino

강남구 신사동 536-3 (가로수길점), 신사동, Seoul

Crafting multiple gelato flavors into the shape of a flower, each layer shaved into thin petals and applied one by one — Amorino offers over twenty gelato flavors, satisfying even the most devoted ice cream enthusiast. **●○ Myeongdong**

All Chinese People

Myeongdong is where Chinese tourists congregate the most. It is home to two major duty-free stores (Lotte and Shinsegae) and is packed with Korean clothing and cosmetics brand shops. Compared to Garosu-gil, Myeongdong is more mid-to-low-end in terms of consumption. Compared to Dongdaemun, it has more retail stores. The shops here are densely packed, and foot traffic is at its peak. Naturally, there is plenty of food to explore, and at night, street stalls emerge — a must for those who love night markets and bustling crowds.

**Samgyetang | Jeonju Jungang Hoegwan**

![Jeonju Jungang Hoegwan location](images/009.png)

Jeonju Jungang Hoegwan, Myeongdong Main Branch

전주중앙회관 명동본점, Seoul

In Myeongdong, we chose Jeonju Jungang Hoegwan, famous for its samgyetang (ginseng chicken soup) and Jeonju-style dolsot bibimbap.

Jeonju Jungang Hoegwan is a decades-old establishment whose delicious bibimbap has been passed down through three generations. Its reputation is well-known not only throughout Korea but also in China, where it frequently appears on TV and in travel food guides. Many people are likely familiar with the name. The awards and certificates displayed on the walls seem to tell the story of Jeonju Jungang Hoegwan's culinary legacy.

Samgyetang is a **traditional Korean dish.** Koreans eat it during the three hottest days of summer — Chobok, Jungbok, and Malbok — believing that fighting heat with heat helps replenish energy and prevent heatstroke and other summer ailments. The classic preparation involves hollowing out a young chicken, stuffing it with glutinous rice and ginseng, and simmering it with jujubes and garlic.

![Samgyetang ginseng chicken soup](images/029.jpg)

The "beef hot pot" here is also distinctive — thin slices of premium beef, cooked in seconds against the scalding pot wall, soaking up the rich broth, incredibly tender and flavorful.

![Beef hot pot](images/030.jpg)

**Dessert | Gusttimo**

Gusttimo

중구 퇴계로 131, 신일빌딩 별관1층, 명동, Seoul

Another dessert shop that has taken creativity to the extreme: take the Parisian macaron, enlarge it, remove the overly sweet filling, and replace it with a variety of ice creams. This is Gusttimo's macaron ice cream. With over 20 ice cream flavors to choose from, it is irresistible for any girl who loves ice cream.

**●○ Samcheong-dong**

Seoul's Nanluoguxiang

Samcheong-dong sits between Gyeongbokgung Palace and Changdeokgung Palace, with Cheongwadae (the Blue House) to the north and Insa-dong to the south. You can start at Gyeongbokgung, stroll into Samcheong-dong's pedestrian street, wander over to Bukchon to see the traditional hanok village, and then continue to Insa-dong and Cheonggyecheon.

Samcheong-dong is **youthful and laid-back.** The streets are lined with galleries, clothing boutiques, cafes, and handmade craft shops. There are also numerous small, themed museums hidden away — a World Ornament Museum, an Owl Museum, a Toy Museum, and more.

**Cafe | Innisfree Green Cafe**

![Innisfree Green Cafe location](images/009.png)

Innisfree Green Cafe

서울특별시 종로구삼청로 104호, Seoul

Innisfree Green Cafe in Samcheong-dong was my favorite cafe of this trip. Born from the Jeju Island cosmetics brand, it brings Jeju's natural philosophy to coffee. The cafe sits on the second floor above the cosmetics store, but its decor is simple and filled with greenery, with comfortable wooden tables and chairs.

![Innisfree Green Cafe interior](images/032.jpg)

If you think I just liked the environment, you would be wrong. The coffee and desserts here are both beautiful and delicious. The purple sweet potato cake is not cloyingly sweet, and the blueberry cheesecake is excellent.

**●○ Apgujeong and Cheongdam-dong**

The Wealthy District

This is where Korea's elite — the wealthy and the celebrities — gather. It boasts luxury shopping streets comparable to Tokyo's Omotesando, the Rodeo Street that appears constantly in Korean dramas, and to the east, Cheongdam-dong's Fashion Street is **lined with world-famous brand boutiques and refined, upscale restaurants.** This is also the famous **plastic surgery street,** where you will often spot women with bandaged faces.

**●○ Special Recommendations**

Worth the Journey, No Matter How Far

**Soy-Marinated Crab | Ilmi Ganjang Gejang**

![Ilmi Ganjang Gejang location](images/009.png)

Ilmi Ganjang Gejang

서울특별시 동대문구 장안2동 367-3, Seoul

Soy-marinated crab has long been part of Korean culinary life, but it was Jun Ji-hyun in *My Love from the Star* who catapulted this traditional dish to pan-Asian fame. Ilmi Ganjang Gejang sits on a street entirely dedicated to soy-marinated crab — a block where locals specifically come to eat it. Absolutely authentic.

![Soy-marinated crab](images/036.jpg)

The crab is fresh and flavorful, and the price is incredibly affordable — more than half the price of what you would pay in Beijing. An absolute steal.

![Soy-marinated crab close-up](images/037.jpg)

**Standing BBQ | Seo Seo Meokja**

![Seo Seo Meokja location](images/009.png)

Seo Seo Meokja

서울특별시 마포구 노고산동 109-69번지, Seoul

Inside the restaurant, over ten silver oil drums are arranged. Guests gather around them, standing as they grill their meat. The star here is grilled beef ribs — tender, juicy, and wildly popular. However, there is no ventilation system, so do not wear anything too formal — the scent of grilled meat will linger long after.

![Standing BBQ restaurant](images/038.jpg)

The restaurant opens at noon and closes when the ingredients sell out, so it is recommended to go before 5 PM.

![Standing BBQ grill](images/039.jpg)

**Fried Chicken | BHC Chicken**

![BHC location](images/009.png)

BHC

성동구 고산자로6가길 44 (행당한신점), Seoul

Another Korean food that swept across Asia thanks to Jun Ji-hyun. Of all the fried chicken I have tried, BHC's is the best — fittingly, she is also the brand's spokesperson. Their "Best-Seller" is especially outstanding: crispy on the outside, tender on the inside, deeply flavorful. With branches everywhere, it is easy to find.

![BHC fried chicken](images/040.jpg)

I have said so much, but truth be told, I also ate my way through Hongdae's night market.
